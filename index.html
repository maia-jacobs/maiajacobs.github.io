About

I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science and Preventive Medicine at Northwestern University, where I direct the NU-PATH Lab. I am also a core faculty member of Northwestern's Center for Behavioral Intervention Technologies, and Center for Human-Computer Interaction+Design.

I study how predictive models shape high-stakes decision-making, and design intelligent tools that promote collaboration and autonomy within these environments, implementing and evaluating these tools in real-world settings. Current projects include:

  • Studying how recommendations shape collaboration and power dynamics in clinical decisions
  • Using community-based participatory research (CBPR) to build AI tools with and for communities
  • Designing tools that adapt to a person's changing needs over time

Much of this work is grounded in clinical and health settings, where AI adoption is outpacing research about its real-world effects, often creating invisible labor for the clinicians and patients who have to interpret, question, or correct what a system tells them.

I earned my PhD in Human-Centered Computing from the Georgia Institute of Technology and completed a postdoctoral fellowship at Harvard's Center for Research on Computation and Society before joining Northwestern in 2021.
