Research

Human-computer interaction methods for technology that keeps pace with people's changing health needs.

Overview

The NU-PATH Lab (Personalized and Adaptive Technology for Health) uses HCI methods to design and evaluate novel technologies to support people's dynamic health needs and goals. The lab's work spans three connected themes:

  • Personalized tools for longitudinal health management — technology that adapts as a person's condition, context, or needs change over months and years.
  • Interactive interfaces for ML-driven clinical decision support — designing how clinicians and patients interact with machine learning predictions in real, time-constrained care settings.
  • Community mental health — understanding mental health challenges in specific communities and co-creating digital solutions with the people who will use them.

Current projects

CASMI: Co-designing Patient-Facing Machine Learning for Prenatal Stress Reduction

Includes patients directly in the development and design of machine-learning prediction tools for prenatal stress.

Co-designing Interventions for Burnout in Frontline Gender-Based Violence Work

A community-based participatory project co-designing interventions with frontline workers at nonprofit gender-based-violence survivor service organizations.

Understanding Self-Reflection Practices

Researching scalable, effective interventions in response to growing mental health concerns.

EquityWare: Designing Equitable Wearables

Democratizing wearable technologies by developing and co-designing inclusive wearables with and for marginalized groups.

Health-AI Decision Support Tools

Examines how healthcare AI systems often fail to account for the socio-technical complexities present in real clinical workflows.

Digital Mental Health Interventions for Youth Learning CS

Designing strategies and interventions that support youth's mental health within computer science education environments.

Team science

Beyond lab-led projects, Maia serves as lead HCI researcher and co-investigator on several large, interdisciplinary NIH-funded initiatives, including the National Cancer Institute's ACCERT Patient Navigation program (PIs: Melissa Simon, Betina Yanez), the STELLAR telehealth cancer-care program (PIs: Sofia Garcia, Brian Hitsman, Siobhan Phillips, Bonnie Spring), and NIMH's Technology Enabled Services for Coordinated Care of Depression program (PI: David Mohr).
